# Publisher terms

By opening a pull request that lists a spore here, you confirm:

1. **You have the right to publish it.** You wrote it, or you have permission, and
   listing it infringes nobody's rights.
2. **It does what its listing says.** Its `description` and declared
   `capabilities` describe its actual behaviour. Understating capabilities to get
   past the consent sheet is grounds for immediate revocation and a publisher ban.
3. **You will disclose what it collects.** A spore that sends anything anywhere
   says so in its README, plainly, including where.
4. **You accept revocation.** A spore found to be malicious, deceptive, or
   dangerously broken will be revoked without notice.
5. **You license the listing metadata** (`index.json` entry) under this
   repository's licence. Your spore keeps whatever licence you give it.

## What the project provides

Nothing. Spores are not reviewed, endorsed, audited, or guaranteed, and are
provided without warranty of any kind. Aneural distributes locations and hashes;
it does not vet behaviour. Users are told this before every install:

> Spores are published by third parties. Aneural does not review, endorse, or
> guarantee them, and they are provided without warranty. Installing a spore means
> you accept its publisher's terms and whatever risk it carries.

## Reporting

Open an issue titled `report: <spore id>`. For anything with a security impact, do
not open a public issue — report it privately at
https://github.com/Parnassix/aneural/security/advisories/new.
